GYCO

The Greater Youth Collaborative Opus is a student-centered youth nonprofit in Georgia. Students rehearse, perform, teach, research, and organize their own community projects.

About GYCO

Greater Youth Collaborative Opus (GYCO) is a student-centered youth nonprofit founded in 2022 by two students and an educator, and officially registered as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization in May 2023.

Built around the idea of "Learn well, Give well", GYCO helps students turn what they learn into meaningful service through music, education, research, storytelling, and student leadership.

Through more than 70 performances, service projects, and community initiatives, students have worked with seniors, patients and families, individuals with disabilities, refugees, unhoused communities, and other community partners.

What began primarily through music has continued to grow into something broader. Students now create educational resources, conduct research inspired by their service experiences, document community stories, develop new outreach projects, and bring those ideas back to their own schools.

One program that grew from these experiences is WE ARE WITH YOU (WAWY), GYCO’s first student-centered platform, proposed and developed by student founders Aaron and Kate and piloted with GYCO students.

After years of performing in different communities, they began asking, “How can we still be with them when we cannot be there in person?” Built around QR-based connections and ongoing partnerships, WAWY extends that connection across distance and time—bringing messages, music, and resources from GYCO students, partner staff, and the wider community to patients and families, seniors, individuals with disabilities, refugees, hospice communities, and others we serve.

Today, GYCO's work is organized around five areas:

Perform · Educate · Research · Connect · Lead

Our Programs

01 Perform

Performance

Music was where GYCO began and remains one of the most direct ways we connect with others.

Students perform for patients and families, senior communities, individuals with disabilities, and community organizations, using music to create moments of encouragement and connection.

Performances range from small community visits to collaborative concerts and special events with partner organizations. Students are encouraged not only to perform well, but also to understand who they are performing for and why their presence matters.

For GYCO, performance is not simply about presenting music. It is about using music as a way to enter a community, listen, build relationships, and share an experience together.

02 Educate

Education

GYCO encourages students to share what they have learned with others.

Students create books, educational materials, teaching videos, digital resources, workshops, and activities that make their knowledge useful to the communities around them.

Projects may involve music, health education, academics, language, creative learning, or other topics that can benefit the communities we work with.

Educational projects should be developed with accessibility and usefulness in mind. When possible, students work with educators, professionals, and partner organizations to make sure resources are appropriate for the people they are designed to serve.

The goal is simple: learning should not stop with the person who learned it.

When students teach, explain, create, and share, they deepen their own understanding while helping someone else gain access to new information or opportunities.

03 Research

Research

Service often creates questions.

GYCO helps students turn questions discovered through community experiences into research that can improve programs and inspire new ideas.

Students may collect observations, review existing research, develop surveys, analyze program outcomes, or work with mentors to better understand the communities and issues connected to their projects.

Research is not treated as something separate from service. Instead, it completes a cycle:

Serve → Observe → Question → Research → Improve

What students learn through research can then be used to strengthen existing programs, develop new projects, and make future community engagement more thoughtful and effective.

04 Connect

Press

Every community has stories worth hearing.

Through GYCO Press, students use interviews, articles, photography, and video to document and connect the people, organizations, schools, and communities involved in our work.

Students may highlight a partner organization, speak with a community leader, document a student project, or share how young people in different places are responding to similar challenges.

Press should not feel like simple promotion or marketing for GYCO. Its purpose is to connect communities through their experiences and perspectives.

By documenting and sharing these stories, students learn how to listen carefully, communicate responsibly, and help voices from different communities reach one another.

05 Lead

GYCO Chapters

GYCO Chapters are student-led groups based in schools and communities. Some are officially recognized school clubs, while others begin as student-led groups connected to GYCO.

Each Chapter can identify local needs, participate in GYCO projects, create its own projects, and remain connected with students across the larger GYCO network.

Rather than simply repeating the same activities everywhere, Chapters give students the opportunity to respond to the needs of their own communities while sharing the same GYCO philosophy:

Learn well, Give well
