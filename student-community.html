GYCO

The Greater Youth Collaborative Opus is a student organization in Georgia, founded in 2023 and registered as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Students rehearse, perform, teach, and organize their own community projects.

What Students Do

Most GYCO students are musicians. They rehearse as an ensemble and perform for community partners — and the work has grown from there.

  • Perform live music at hospitals, family houses, and senior communities
  • Teach — younger students, teaching videos, and Beat & Breeze sessions
  • Write letters and prepare care packages
  • Plan and run projects with partner organizations

Where GYCO Has Served

Students return to the same communities rather than visiting once. So far that has included:

  • City of Hope Atlanta (CTCA)
  • Ronald McDonald House Charities of Atlanta
  • Senior living communities
  • The America Wheat Mission (Milal)
  • Veterans — letters, messages, and care packages
  • Refugee families, in partnership with Friends of Refugees
  • People experiencing homelessness in Atlanta — music and 100 care packages

Programs

WE ARE WITH YOU is GYCO's main community initiative — the performances, letters, and partner pages across this site. Within it, students run several ongoing series:

Winds of Love Performances at hospitals, family houses, senior communities, and community concerts. Photos →
Taps of Love Short teaching videos created by students — rhythms, instruments, and simple activities. Learn more →
Voices of Love Community members share the story or memory behind a song; students perform it when possible. Learn more →
Circle of Love Family programs, volunteer projects, and partnership work with community organizations. Get involved →

GYCO numbers its larger bodies of work like a composer numbers an opus. WE ARE WITH YOU is OPUS 1; NADO School is OPUS 2; student research, student press, school clubs (SHAN & ECHO), and new creative projects continue the series.

How Students Learn

GYCO uses the NADO School approach: learn a skill, make it your own, use it to help someone else, and keep growing from there. New students don't need experience — curiosity and commitment are enough.

Students lead the work themselves: choosing repertoire, planning visits, producing videos, and building relationships with partners.
